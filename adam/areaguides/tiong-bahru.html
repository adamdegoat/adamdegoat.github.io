Tiong Bahru

Singapore's oldest housing estate, the low-rise pre-war walk-ups with the curved Streamline Moderne fronts, now a conservation area and one of the most sought-after foodie addresses on the city fringe.

What it's like

Tiong Bahru is where buyers pay up for character, low-rise heritage and a central address, and where the conserved walk-ups draw a steady stream of expat renters.

Tiong Bahru is Singapore's oldest housing estate, built by the Singapore Improvement Trust before the war, and it still looks the part. The low-rise blocks with their curved corners, rounded balconies and long horizontal lines are Streamline Moderne, a 1930s style you will not find anywhere else on the island at this scale. The old core is a conservation area, so those walk-ups are protected and cannot be torn down or built over. That single fact is what gives the estate its look and its pull: three and four storey blocks, mature trees, quiet internal lanes, and none of the tower-and-podium sameness of a newer town.

The housing here is not one thing. The conserved SIT walk-ups are mostly two and three room units, they have no lift, no carpark and no facilities, and they rarely come up for sale, so a lot of them are rented out, often to expats who want the character and the location. The $999k four-room figure comes from the newer stock, the HDB blocks towards Boon Tiong Road and Jalan Membina that were built long after the pre-war core. On top of that sits a layer of private condos, Highline Residences on the old Zion Road site being the headline name, which is why a heritage walk-up, a newer flat and a two-million-dollar condo can all sit within a few streets. The estate is also known for what moved in over the last fifteen years: the cafes, the bakeries, Tiong Bahru Bakery, the bookshops and small shops that turned it into a lifestyle destination and pushed prices up with it.

Getting to town is easy. Tiong Bahru MRT on the East-West Line puts you a few stops from the CBD and the Orchard fringe, and plenty of people here simply walk or cycle in. Day to day, Tiong Bahru Market and Food Centre is the anchor, one of the better-known hawker centres in the country for chwee kueh and lor mee, with the wet market below. The honest catch is that you pay a clear premium for all of this, and the walk-ups in particular ask a lot for what they physically give you: no lift, no parking, shorter remaining leases and small layouts. Weekends bring crowds and a real fight for parking, and if you need a big flat for a large family, the stock here is thin. You buy in Tiong Bahru for the character and the address, not for space or a bargain.

Who it suitsBuyers and renters who want character, low-rise heritage and a central foodie address, and will pay up for it, plus investors targeting the strong expat rental demand on the conserved walk-ups.

The honest view

What works

  • Real character and heritage, the conserved Streamline Moderne walk-ups are protected and cannot be redeveloped, which keeps the estate low-rise and distinctive.
  • Genuinely central, with Tiong Bahru MRT on the East-West Line a few stops from the CBD, and Tiong Bahru Market plus the cafes and bakeries on the doorstep.
  • Strong rental demand, especially from expats who want the walk-ups, which supports a healthier HDB yield near 4.7% than most central towns.

Worth weighing

  • The conserved walk-ups have no lift, no carpark and no facilities, with shorter remaining leases, and they ask a high price for small, older layouts.
  • You pay a clear premium across the board, and weekends bring crowds and a real fight for parking around the cafes and market.
  • Large-family stock is limited, so households needing a big flat will find the choice here thin.

Tiong Bahru

新加坡历史最悠久的住宅区，二战前建造的低层无电梯公寓，外立面带有标志性的流线弧形设计，如今已是保育区，也是城市边缘最受追捧的美食地址之一。

这里是什么样

Tiong Bahru 就是那种买家愿意为个性、低层老建筑和中心地段多付一些的地方，受保育的步行楼也一直吸引着一批又一批稳定的外籍租客。

Tiong Bahru 是新加坡最老的住宅区，战前由 Singapore Improvement Trust 建造，至今风貌依旧。低层楼栋带着圆角、弧形阳台和横向长线条，是 1930 年代的 Streamline Moderne 风格，这种规模的建筑在岛上找不到第二处。旧核心区是保育区，那些步行楼受到保护，不能拆除或重建。正是这一点，赋予了这个住宅区独特的面貌和吸引力：三四层楼的建筑、成熟的大树、幽静的内街，没有那种新市镇常见的高楼加裙楼的千篇一律。

这里的住宅不是单一类型的。受保育的 SIT 步行楼多为两房和三房，没有电梯、没有停车场，也没有配套设施，而且很少放出来卖，很多都出租给想要这里个性和地段的外籍人士。$999k 的四房数字来自较新的 HDB 楼栋，也就是战前核心区建成很久之后才兴建的 Boon Tiong Road 和 Jalan Membina 一带。在这之上，还有一层私人 condo，最具代表性的是旧 Zion Road 地段上的 Highline Residences，这也解释了为什么一栋老步行楼、一套新 HDB 和一间两百万的 condo 可以在几条街内并存。这个住宅区过去十五年里还因另一件事出名：陆续开进来的咖啡馆、面包店、Tiong Bahru Bakery、书店和小店，把这里变成了一个生活品味目的地，也把价格一起带了上去。

出行很方便。Tiong Bahru MRT 在 East-West Line 上，坐几站就到 CBD 和 Orchard 边缘，很多住在这里的人干脆走路或骑车进城。日常生活的中心是 Tiong Bahru Market and Food Centre，是全岛出名的熟食中心之一，水粿和卤面都有名气，楼下还有巴刹。说实话，代价也是明摆着的：你要为这一切付出明显的溢价，步行楼尤其如此，没有电梯、没有停车位、剩余地契较短、户型也小。周末人多，停车位是真正的抢手货，如果你需要一套大单位供一大家子住，这里的选择很少。买 Tiong Bahru，买的是个性和地段，不是空间，也不是捡便宜。

适合谁想要个性、低层老建筑和中心美食地段、愿意为此多付的买家和租客，以及看中受保育步行楼强劲外籍租赁需求的投资者。

老实说

好在哪里

  • 真实的个性与历史底蕴，受保育的Streamline Moderne步梯楼受到保护，不能重新发展，让整个屋苑保持低层风貌，别具一格。
  • 地段真的很中心，Tiong Bahru MRT在东西线上，离CBD就几站路，出门就是Tiong Bahru Market，咖啡馆和面包店都在脚边。
  • 租赁需求强劲，尤其是想住步梯楼的外籍人士，这支撑了HDB租金回报率接近4.7%，比大多数中部市镇来得健康。

需要权衡

  • 受保育的步梯楼没有电梯、没有停车场、没有设施，剩余租约也较短，却要价不菲，户型又小又旧。
  • 整体来说溢价明显，周末人潮涌现，咖啡馆和市场周围停车位真的很难抢。
  • 大户型房源有限，需要大单位的家庭在这里选择不多。
